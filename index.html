Questions, answered

The things every tired mama asks

Why does my newborn only sleep when held?

It's biology, not a bad habit. Newborns are neurologically immature and can't yet regulate their own temperature, heart rate, or stress hormones — being held does it for them. Contact napping is developmentally normal. The goal isn't to stop holding your baby, but to gently teach their nervous system it's safe to be put down.

What is matrescence?

Matrescence is the developmental transition of becoming a mother — a profound neurological, hormonal, and identity shift comparable in scale to adolescence. Research shows the maternal brain physically rewires and these changes persist for at least two years. Feeling like you've lost yourself isn't failure; it's a recognised, normal part of this transition.

How do I respond to a toddler meltdown without yelling?

During a meltdown a toddler's thinking brain goes offline, so reasoning doesn't work. Use a four-part script: acknowledge the feeling, hold the boundary calmly, offer a small choice, then follow through warmly. Connect before you correct — a child who feels understood can settle; a child who feels judged escalates.

Does a screen-time detox have to be cold turkey?

No — and cold turkey usually backfires. Abruptly removing a dopamine trigger causes a real withdrawal-style meltdown. A gradual, four-week reduction lets the brain readjust, builds your child's buy-in, and gives you time to make real life more interesting. Consistency, not strictness, is what makes it stick.

How do I help a child with big emotions?

Name the feeling before fixing the behaviour — affect labelling measurably lowers a feeling's intensity in the brain. Give them precise emotion words, build a calm-down corner (a self-soothing space, not a punishment), and model regulating your own feelings. Big feelings aren't a defect; the missing skill is regulation, and it's teachable.

How do I know if I have postpartum depression or baby blues?

Baby blues affect up to 80% of mothers, start 2-3 days after birth, and lift within two weeks. Postpartum depression affects about 1 in 5, can begin any time in the first year, and doesn't resolve on its own — but it responds well to treatment. Persistent sadness, numbness, or rage beyond two weeks warrants a check-in.
